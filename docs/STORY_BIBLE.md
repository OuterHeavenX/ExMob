# EXMOB - STORY BIBLE

## Themes

- "You can leave the organization, but the organization never leaves you."
- "What does freedom cost?"
- "Can money actually protect you?"
- "How much violence follows a violent life?"

Tone: crime, betrayal, dark humor, paranoia, loyalty, regret, survival, wealth, consequence.
The story must remain understandable. No sprawling lore. A man, a family he left, a ledger, and a
price on his head.

## The protagonist

**Ray Moretti** (game title remains EXMOB; "ExMob" is what the Family's people call him on the
phone - the ex-mob guy, the one who walked).

- Age 54. Twenty-nine years inside the Vescari Family. Started as a driver, became a collector,
  then the Family's fixer: the man sent when a problem needed to disappear cleanly.
- Calm under pressure. Dry humor. Talks little. Notices everything.
- Not a superhero. Broad, heavy, a little slow to get up off the floor. Broken nose from a life
  before the suits. Gray at the temples. Reading glasses in a pocket he pretends not to need.
- Visual silhouette: long dark wool overcoat over a knit sweater at the Cabin; broad shoulders,
  slight forward lean, one hand always near a pocket. Later chapters move him into tailored suits
  without losing the coat silhouette.
- Habit: he counts. Rounds, exits, seconds, cars. It is how he stays alive and it is how the HUD
  should feel - precise and unhurried.

### Why he left

Ray was ordered to clean up a loose end: Danny Kessler, the Family's young bookkeeper, son of
Ray's late partner Mike Kessler. Danny had seen the wrong ledger. Ray drove Danny to the river,
sat with him for an hour, and let him run. Then Ray took the ledger himself.

The ledger - "the book" - is decades of the Family's payments, judges, cops, unions, and bodies.
Ray did not take it to trade. He took it so that nobody could send another kid to the river for
seeing it. He tells himself that is the reason. It is at least half true.

### What he wants

To be left alone. To sit on a porch. To not count anymore. He learns across the campaign that
being left alone is not something you can buy, and that every dollar he spends making himself
safer makes him easier to find.

## The organization

**The Vescari Family** (fictional). Old money crime. Construction, waste, ports, unions, gambling.
Run by **Carmine Vescari**, 71, who genuinely liked Ray and takes the betrayal personally.

Key figures (referenced in phone calls, notes, and later chapters; only voices and names in the
Cabin):

- **Carmine Vescari** - the boss. Patient. Sentimental about loyalty. Places the contract.
- **Sal Brancusi** - underboss. Efficient, cold, the one actually running the hunt. Becomes the
  Underboss encounter in later chapters.
- **Teddy "Two Coats" Marchese** - a captain who came up with Ray. Sends the first crews to the
  Cabin because he assumes Ray has gone soft. He is wrong, and it costs him.
- **Danny Kessler** - the kid Ray let go. Alive. Off-screen in Chapter 1. Becomes an ally / source
  in later chapters and the reason Ray cannot simply hand the book back.
- **"The Clerk"** - a nameless professional contract killer the Family hires when their own crews
  keep failing. Reserved for the finale arc.

## Campaign structure

Each chapter is a property. Each property is a chapter of Ray's life getting bigger and more
visible. Each chapter ends with the location compromised beyond repair.

### Chapter 1 - The Cabin ("They Found You")

Remote wilderness, cheap, quiet, primitive. Ray has been here four months. Cash in a coffee can.
A burner phone he never turns on. A pistol under the pillow he hasn't touched in weeks.

Opening: black screen, wind, trees, a distant engine. Fade in on the cabin interior at night. Ray
is at the table with a cup of coffee and a newspaper clipping he has read a hundred times. The
burner phone, which should be off, lights up. One message: **"ray. its teddy. we know where the
porch is. sorry."**

Headlights through the trees. An old sedan stops at the end of the driveway. Doors open. Three
men get out. Gameplay begins.

Five waves. Teddy's people. The first ones are street goons who think this is easy money. By the
end, Teddy sends a real crew with a hitman. After the fifth wave the cabin is riddled, the door is
gone, and Ray hears more engines on the county road. He takes the coffee can, the book, and the
car. **CABIN COMPROMISED.**

Environmental storytelling in the Cabin: the clipping ("FEDERAL PROBE INTO VESCARI CONSTRUCTION
STALLS"), a packed suitcase by the bed, cash in the coffee can, the burner phone, the pistol,
a photograph of two men on a fishing boat (Ray and Mike Kessler, years ago), unopened mail
addressed to a name that is not his, a county map with two routes marked and one crossed out.

### Chapter 2 - The Small House ("New Name")

Another state. Modest neighborhood. Ray thinks he can disappear. He pays cash for a house with a
fence and a dog next door. He is wrong: a plate reader at a toll plaza puts him in the county.
The Family sends better people and, for the first time, a vehicle-based drive-by. Ray learns he
cannot outrun the book; he starts using it.

### Chapter 3 - The Townhouse ("Leverage")

City. Ray has money now, some of it from selling pages of the ledger to people who need to know
what is in them. Neighbors, streets, alleys, rooftops. Attack directions multiply. Sal Brancusi
takes direct control of the hunt. Danny Kessler resurfaces.

### Chapter 4 - The Luxury Home ("Visible")

Ray stops hiding and starts living. Gated community. Cameras. Hired muscle. The Family responds
with professionals: Cleaners, snipers, arsonists. The bounty goes public within the underworld.
Carmine calls Ray personally. It is the one conversation in the game where both men are honest.

### Chapter 5 - The Estate ("Perimeter")

Large property, multiple buildings, long perimeter, gates, staff, guards. Ray has built a small
kingdom. The Family attacks the way you attack a kingdom: from every side, with vehicles and a
captain coordinating. Sal Brancusi comes himself. The Underboss encounter ends the chapter.

### Final Chapter - The Mansion ("Nowhere Else")

Massive luxury property. Ray has everything. Carmine decides incremental hit squads are over. The
Family sends everyone, and The Clerk. Security fails, gates fall, guards are overwhelmed, windows
shatter, vehicles flood the grounds, the attack moves floor by floor, the mansion becomes a ruin
around him.

Ending direction (to be finalized in SCRIBE pass, kept original): Ray survives the assault in a
mansion that is no longer a home. The last scene mirrors the first: a table, a cup of coffee,
a phone that lights up. This time it is Danny. Ray turns the phone off. Whether he stays or goes
is left to the player's final choice, with the book either burned or mailed to a federal address
written on the county map from Chapter 1.

Rules for the finale: no copied dialogue, characters, mansion layout, or scenes from any film. It
evokes the fantasy of a desperate last stand; it is built entirely from EXMOB's own beats
(the coffee can, the book, the phone, the porch).

## Dialogue style

Sparse. Short lines. Ray speaks in fragments. Dark humor lands dry, never quippy.

Examples (original):

- "Teddy always did send the cheap ones first."
- "Four guys. One car. Somebody's saving on gas."
- "You count the exits. Then you count them again."
- Enemy bark: "He's in the back! Kick it!"
- Enemy bark: "Boss said alive if easy. It ain't easy."

## Naming

Working title EXMOB stays as the game title. Ray Moretti is the character. The Family is the
Vescari Family. All names are fictional and unrelated to real persons or organizations.
